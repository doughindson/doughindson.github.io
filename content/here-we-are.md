# Apple TV+ — Here We Are

[Video — video-01-2f630a9c-fc69-4ea1-901e-bc47f71bd05c.mp4 (Squarespace-hosted, 99s)](./here-we-are/video-01-2f630a9c-fc69-4ea1-901e-bc47f71bd05c.mp4)

*Role:* Concept Design

*Studio:* StudioAKA

*Client:* Apple TV+

'HERE WE ARE: Notes For Living On Planet Earth', is based on the No. 1 New York Times Bestseller and 2017's No. 1 TIME Best Book of the Year by Oliver Jeffers and follows Finn; a precocious child who, on Earth Day, learns about the wonders of the planet from his parents — and from a mysterious exhibit at the aptly titled Museum of Everything.

I joined the project early on, helping to translate Oliver Jeffers’ beautiful hand-drawn line-work into expanded environments and props, that would then be recreated in 3D.

Although the approach used a lot of traditional mark making techniques, like pencil lines and brush strokes, the 3D models would be lit realistically — so the concept design needed to really emphasise the lighting and sense of space.

![](./here-we-are/17-02-Kitchen_Concept.jpg)
![](./here-we-are/18-HLM_Concept_Neighbourhood_Block_Bridge.v001.jpg)
![](./here-we-are/19-HLM_Neighbourhood_TrainStation_Front.v001.jpg)

The original book had plenty of source material to draw from, however many of the scenes in the film were completely new and required a great deal of research. Looking for aspects from New York streets, brownstone apartments and aging museums, to plan layouts and understand what the camera would see from each angle.

Many of the original drawings simply wouldn’t have been able to exist in a fully dimensional space, so the challenge was to incorporate the charming wonkiness of of the line-work with layouts that would work in-camera. The end result being a blend of warm human details, and a natural sense of space and light.
